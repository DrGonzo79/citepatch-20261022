## Buyer, pain, and gap

The buyer is a solo founder or small B2B SaaS growth team that sees high-converting visits from AI assistants but cannot justify enterprise visibility tooling. The painful status quo is a site-level mention chart that confirms a competitor won without telling the founder which money page failed or what to change. The narrow gap is a page-type filter for pricing, documentation, and comparison pages plus a concrete repair brief.

The report argues that AI-originated visitors can convert materially better than ordinary organic traffic while citations rotate rapidly: only 10.6% of tracked URLs survived 28 days in one cited study, with other studies reporting 69–74% source rotation over shorter periods. That volatility supports recurring monitoring but also makes attribution and retention fragile.

## Market and evidence

- 14,470 monthly US searches were reported across 11 GEO/AEO/LLM SEO category and solution terms, with weighted CPC above $35. The report separately shows 14.8K/mo for “generative engine optimization” (down 45% YoY), 823K/mo for broad “seo tools” (up 648%), 8.1K/mo for “seo analyzer” (down 64%), and 18.1K/mo for “seo keyword analysis” (flat). Broad-keyword growth is not proof of demand for this wedge.
- Two cited educational videos had roughly 107K and 159K views, supporting creator/content interest.
- Reddit and social posts show founders testing their own brands, seeing competitors recommended, and objecting to $499–$699 monthly tools. These are directional demand receipts, not verified willingness to pay for CitePatch.
- The category has at least 30 trackers. Mapped competitors include Profound, Peec AI, Otterly, AthenaHQ, Semrush, Ahrefs Brand Radar, ZipTie, and Orchly.
- Price anchors in the report: Otterly $29 entry, ZipTie about $35–$69, Orchly $49, Peec AI $95/$245/$495, Semrush $99 per domain, AthenaHQ $295, and expensive Ahrefs/Profound configurations. Prices can change and require live verification before commercial use.

## Wedge, offer ladder, and GTM

The wedge is a “Fix My Pricing Page” audit: compare one URL across 25 prompts and four engines, show the competitor pages cited instead, and return six prioritized edits. The proposed ladder is free single-page audit → $499 one-off report for pricing/docs/comparison pages → $49/month Solo tracker → $149/month Studio tracker. The report models 80 free audits, 20 Solo subscribers, three Studio subscribers, and ten one-off audits for about $9K booked in 90 days; most conversion assumptions are unverified.

Recommended first channel: 60 days of helpful participation in AEO/SaaS communities plus personalized Loom audits to 500 founders. Paid search is unattractive against venture-backed incumbents. The demand test should measure completed audits, calls, and explicit acceptance of $499/$49 offers—not traffic alone.

## Risks and strategic read

The page-type filter is copyable; Peec already classifies source types, Ahrefs filters by path, ZipTie has URL-level filtering, and Orchly exposes mention gaps. The defensible artifact must become the rewrite rubric and the longitudinal experiment data connecting edits to citation movement. High citation variance can make recommendations appear ineffective. Scraping costs, engine terms, prompt representativeness, and false causal claims are material technical and trust risks.

## Build interpretation

The prototype therefore does not pretend to query live engines. It demonstrates the useful last mile: select a pricing/docs/comparison page, inspect citation gaps, and work through a deterministic repair queue. Shared fictional fixtures make the behavior explicit and testable. A real product would add scheduled collectors, evidence snapshots, confidence intervals, experiment cohorts, and human-reviewed repair rules before adding generative rewrite suggestions.
